### Summary

As healthcare systems increasingly integrate artificial intelligence (AI) to manage patient communications, understanding patient perceptions of AI-generated responses becomes crucial. Recent studies, including a comprehensive survey conducted by Duke University Health System, reveal a nuanced landscape: while AI-generated messages are often rated higher for empathy and detail, patient satisfaction diminishes when the AI authorship is disclosed. This article delves into these findings, exploring the implications for healthcare providers aiming to balance technological efficiency with patient trust and satisfaction.

### Introduction

The advent of AI in healthcare promises enhanced efficiency, particularly in managing the burgeoning volume of patient-clinician communications via electronic health record (EHR) portals. However, the integration of AI into such sensitive areas necessitates a thorough understanding of patient perceptions and the ethical considerations involved.

---

### The Rise of AI in Patient Communications

Healthcare providers are increasingly deploying AI to draft responses to patient inquiries, aiming to alleviate clinician workload and expedite communication. These AI systems, often powered by large language models, can generate responses that are detailed and, at times, exhibit a degree of empathy. Yet, the reception of these AI-generated messages by patients remains a critical factor in their successful implementation.

---

### Insights from the Duke University Survey

A pivotal study conducted by Duke University Health System surveyed 1,455 patients to assess their reactions to AI-generated responses in patient portals. Participants were presented with 18 scenarios varying in clinical seriousness, response authorship (AI or human), and disclosure of authorship. The findings were illuminating:

- **Preference for AI Content:** AI-generated messages were often preferred for their length, detail, and perceived empathy.
- **Impact of Disclosure:** Satisfaction levels dropped when patients were informed that the message was AI-generated, compared to when the message was attributed to a human or when no authorship information was provided.
- **Perception of Care:** Patients felt more cared for when messages were believed to be written by clinicians, highlighting the importance of perceived human involvement in healthcare communications.

  **Findings:** This survey study of 1455 respondents showed that while overall satisfaction was high (>75%) regardless of author, respondents preferred responses written by AI over those written by a human (mean difference, 0.30 points on a 5-point Likert scale for satisfaction). However, when an AI author was disclosed, satisfaction was lower for AI compared with a human author (mean difference, 0.13 points).

  **Meaning:** Reduced satisfaction due to AI disclosure should be balanced with the importance of patient autonomy and empowerment.

---

### Ethical Considerations and Patient Trust

The decrease in satisfaction upon disclosure of AI authorship raises ethical questions about transparency and trust. Patients may feel deceived or perceive a lack of personal care when informed that an AI, rather than their clinician, authored a message. This underscores the delicate balance healthcare providers must maintain between leveraging AI for efficiency and preserving the human touch that is central to patient care.

---

### Implications for Healthcare Providers

Healthcare systems must navigate the integration of AI in patient communications thoughtfully:

- **Transparent Communication:** Clearly communicating the role of AI in drafting messages, while ensuring clinician oversight, can help maintain trust.
- **Clinician Involvement:** Involving clinicians in reviewing and personalizing AI-generated messages may enhance patient satisfaction and trust.
- **Patient Education:** Educating patients about the use of AI in healthcare and its benefits can mitigate concerns and foster acceptance.

---

### Conclusion

The integration of AI into patient-clinician communications offers promising benefits in terms of efficiency and workload management. However, patient perceptions play a crucial role in the successful adoption of such technologies. Ensuring transparency, maintaining clinician involvement, and prioritizing patient trust are essential strategies for healthcare providers as they navigate this evolving landscape.
