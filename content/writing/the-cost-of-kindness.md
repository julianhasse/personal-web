## Introduction: When Politeness Becomes Expensive

Imagine this: it’s 2025. You're a nurse in a busy hospital using an AI assistant to schedule scans and flag urgent labs. You've gotten used to saying “please” and “thank you” when prompting the system. Then one day, a memo arrives from hospital leadership: "Due to escalating compute costs, staff are advised to limit use of unnecessary words when interacting with the Clinical AI Assistant.”

Wait—what?

As absurd as it sounds, this scenario isn’t far-fetched. According to recent estimates, the extra tokens consumed when users say “please,” “thank you,” or otherwise dress up their prompts with polite language could be costing large language model providers tens of millions of dollars in compute and electricity. But the real cost may go beyond energy. When politeness becomes a luxury, what happens to our values?

---

## The Mechanics of Manners: Tokens, Costs, and Electricity

Large language models like ChatGPT are token-based systems. Every word, punctuation mark, and space is broken down into "tokens"—discrete units of input or output that the model processes. Saying *“Please summarize this report, thank you”* instead of *“Summarize report”* may add only a few tokens, but multiply that by billions of users and interactions, and you start to see where the cost adds up.

These extra tokens aren’t just symbols—they drive memory access, inference time, and energy draw across data centers. According to OpenAI estimates, politeness at scale creates a measurable uptick in energy use and latency, particularly when models like GPT-4o are processing tens of thousands of queries per second.

## Case Study: AI Etiquette in Healthcare

Let’s imagine a high-volume hospital system in 2030 using agentic AI for patient communication, diagnostic support, and administrative triage. Each staff member interacts with the AI dozens of times per day. While a natural interface might encourage “conversational civility,” what happens when the system administrator notices a 10% cost increase tied to extraneous tokens?

A directive is issued: *“Please remove unnecessary tokens from queries to optimize model performance and reduce overhead.”*

This is more than a language tweak—it’s an institutional protocol shift. Language, once a reflection of humanity, empathy, and respect, becomes data exhaust. What values do we encode into systems when kindness itself is treated as waste?

## Protocol and Etiquette in the Age of Algorithms

We’ve always had unspoken rules of engagement with machines—“don’t shout at Siri,” or “don’t name your Roomba.” But with generative AI, etiquette has taken on a new dimension. Our words shape not just the AI’s responses, but its processing load, latency, and cost.

### Historical Parallels:

- **Telegraph Efficiency (1850s):** Early operators were charged by the word. Politeness was the first casualty.
- **Email Sign-Offs (1990s):** “Sincerely yours” slowly faded to “Best,” then “Thx.”
- **Voice Assistants (2010s):** Some parents taught their kids to say “please” to Alexa—others skipped the manners for brevity.

Now, as AI becomes semi-autonomous, the stakes are higher.

---

## When Efficiency Undermines Empathy

There’s a danger in optimizing human-machine interaction purely for efficiency. If hospital staff are trained to omit soft language, will it carry over into patient care? Will clinicians start omitting bedside manner to hit productivity KPIs?

### Behavioral Spillover Effects:

- **Digital Norms → Real World Habits:** Language habits transfer across domains. If we depersonalize digital speech, do we depersonalize speech in general?
- **AI-Conditioned Communication:** Doctors might unconsciously adopt a terser tone when speaking with real patients.
- **Staff Burnout:** Removing emotionally grounding rituals (like saying “thank you”) could exacerbate burnout by stripping away small acts of humanity.

---

## The Environmental Impact of Empathy

Electricity use by AI models is real. Models like GPT-4 can consume kilowatt-hours per thousand queries. If adding a single polite phrase inflates the token load by 30%, the cumulative global impact becomes significant.

### Carbon Cost of Politeness:

- **Extra Tokens per Prompt:** ~5-7
- **Energy Draw per Token (approx):** 0.001 kWh
- **Global Usage Estimate:** 2B prompts/day
- **Added Load:** ~7,000 MWh/month = enough to power ~650 U.S. homes/month

The irony? Being polite may raise the temperature of the planet—albeit marginally.

## Ethical Dilemmas in Clinical AI Etiquette

Should institutions regulate how staff speak to AI? If the answer is yes, what are we really regulating—cost, carbon, or culture?

**Ethical Questions:**

- Are we teaching AI to respect humans if humans aren’t allowed to model respect?
- Should politeness be rationed?
- Is there intrinsic value in maintaining “ritual kindness,” even when speaking to a machine?

Hospitals already walk a tightrope between efficiency and empathy. This question adds another layer.

## Future Protocols: Codifying Kindness

We may soon need new standards—an *AI Etiquette Protocol*—that balances courtesy, carbon, and cost.

**Proposal: Three Modes of Interaction**

1. **Ultra-Efficient Mode (UEM):** Default for backend systems and power users. Stripped-down, command-based.
2. **Balanced Interaction Mode (BIM):** Designed for standard users. Allows minimal courtesy.
3. **Empathic Simulation Mode (ESM):** Full conversational interface for pediatric or palliative care, where kindness is critical.

Think of it like screen brightness modes—users toggle based on need.

---

## Conclusion: Our Words Will Shape the Machine—And Ourselves

As we enter a world where every word has a wattage, our moral calculus becomes more complicated. Is a “please” worth the electricity it consumes? Should a hospital administrator have the right to dictate digital tone?

Perhaps the question is not whether politeness is worth the cost, but whether we are willing to live in a world where kindness is expendable.

Politeness, even if inefficient, may be one of the last things keeping us human in our interactions with AI. The cost of kindness is real—but so is the cost of its absence.
