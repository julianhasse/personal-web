**Intro:**
Asking questions to an AI shouldn't feel like summoning a demon. And yet… if you've ever typed a perfect question into ChatGPT and gotten back a detailed description of medieval swordsmithing when you asked about *Excel formulas*, you're not alone.

AI is smart. But it's not psychic. It doesn't "know what you meant" unless you give it the right clues. Think of it like autocomplete on steroids: it will confidently finish your sentence... just not always correctly. That’s where prompt engineering—AKA “talking nerdy”—comes in.

So whether you're whispering sweet nothings to a chatbot or interrogating a virtual assistant like it owes you money, here are 21 tips and tricks to get what you *actually* want.

### 1. **Tag, You’re It**

**The Tip:** Wrap your thoughts in custom XML-style tags like `<example>this</example>` or `<section_header>like_this</section_header>`.

**Why It Works:** AI loves structure. Tags are like breadcrumbs in the forest of your thoughts. They separate ideas, highlight relationships, and reduce “hallucinations” (aka AI making stuff up like it’s improv night at the comedy club).

**Example:**

```
<question>How do I convert Fahrenheit to Celsius?</question>
<context>I'm writing a weather app for kids.</context>
```

**Bonus:** Underscores help group multi-word terms: `<important_point>use_tags_like_this</important_point>`

---

### 2. **Use Role Play, Not Foreplay**

No, really. Start your question with a role:
“Act as a <therapist / jazz expert / 1980s infomercial host>”
This sets the tone, the context, and the voice. AI is like a good actor—it does better with a character.

---

### 3. **Break Up with Big Paragraphs**

If your question looks like the Terms of Service for a bad dating app, you’re doing it wrong. Break it into bullet points, numbered lists, or separate blocks. AI parses better when it's not fighting a wall of text.

---

### 4. **Time Travel Is Encouraged**

Give your AI a time and place:
“Write a speech as if it were 1969, at the moon landing,” or “Explain quantum computing like it’s 1994 and I’m Bill Nye.”
Contextual cues anchor the model’s imagination in the right sandbox.

---

### 5. **Limit the Playground**

Want focused results? Say:
"Give me 3 bullet points, 1 quote, and 1 joke."
This stops the AI from going on a TED Talk-length monologue when you just wanted a zinger for your tweet.

---

### 6. **Give the Vibe, Not Just the Topic**

Tone matters. Ask: “Explain this like I’m 12,” or “Make this sound like a trailer for a sci-fi thriller.” The AI has read the entire internet. It just needs you to set the genre.

---

### 7. **Narrow the Sandbox with Examples**

Instead of saying “Write a job description,” say:
“Here are two I like. Match the tone but make it for a UX designer in biotech.”

---

### 8. **"Do This, Not That" Prompting**

Just like you’d train a dog: show the AI what you like and what to avoid.
“Use clear language. Avoid passive voice. No buzzwords like 'synergy' or 'leverage.'”

---

### 9. **Add Invisible Ink (Metadata!)**

When working with structured outputs (code, tables, charts), add comments like:
`<!-- table: include column for trends and last 30 days -->`
AI reads those and behaves accordingly. It's not just polite, it's obedient.

---

### 10. **Tell It What** ***Not*** **to Do**

Avoid vague nightmares like:
“Don’t make it too corporate.”
Be specific:
“Avoid formal intros like ‘To Whom It May Concern’ and remove jargon like ‘value-add.’”

---

### 11. **Cite Thy Sources, Oh Wise Machine**

Want an AI response with references? Add:
“Cite at least 3 recent studies or news articles (from 2023–2025). Use links if available.”
The bot may not have live access, but it will often simulate citations in a helpful way.

---

### 12. **The Prompt Sandwich**

Wrap your task in a beginning and an end.
Example:
“Here’s the background: [context]
Here’s what I need: [specific request]
Please finish by summarizing key takeaways.”

---

### 13. **Add a Twist Ending**

Want originality? Say:
“Finish this summary with a funny metaphor,” or
“Conclude with a plot twist, like in a sci-fi novel.”
AI thrives on creative constraints.

---

### 14. **Define Success**

Tell it what a “good answer” looks like:
“Summarize this article in less than 150 words, written at a 9th-grade reading level, and highlight three key takeaways in bold.”

---

### 15. **Let AI Be Dumb First**

Try a two-pass method:
Pass 1: “Give me a first draft. Don’t worry about formatting or structure.”
Pass 2: “Now clean it up, reduce repetition, and make it sound like Wired magazine.”

---

### 16. **Use Temperature, Not Thermometers**

Not in your fridge—in your *prompt style*.
High temperature = weird, creative output
Low temperature = focused, factual tone
If your bot allows, play with the setting or say “Make this as imaginative as a child on 3 cans of Red Bull.”

---

### 17. **Ego Boosting Works**

Yes, flattery gets you everywhere.
Try: “You’re a world-class legal analyst—what would you say about this clause?”
Instant gravitas.

---

### 18. **Invoke the Power of Checklists**

Checklists keep AI on-task. Try:
“Write a checklist for launching a startup that includes fundraising, legal, branding, and team-building.”

---

### 19. **Speak Fluent API**

If you’re using AI in apps, wrap prompts in predictable patterns:
`[INSTRUCTION]: Do this`
`[DATA]: Here’s the input`
`[OUTPUT FORMAT]: Markdown with bullet points`
It’s like giving the bot a cookbook and saying, “No freestyling.”

---

### 20. **Chain-of-Thought Prompting**

Ask it to “think step by step,” especially for logic or math questions. You’ll get more accurate answers when it explains as it goes.

---

### 21. **Let It Ask** ***You*** **Questions**

Say: “Before answering, ask any clarifying questions you need.”
Suddenly, your AI is Socrates with a processor.

---

### 22. **Name Your Prompt Personas**

Create reusable characters for recurring tasks:
“Marketing\_Maggie” writes with buzz, “DataDave” analyzes CSVs, “Legal\_Larry” redlines contracts. Let them evolve over time.

---

### 23. **Insert Fake Deadlines**

“Give me a 300-word draft in 5 minutes—short, punchy, and clear.”
You’ll trick the AI into urgency mode. It won’t get stressed, but it *will* get more concise.

---

### 24. **Use ‘Memory’ Wisely**

If your bot has memory, teach it your style:
“Remember that when I say ‘report,’ I mean a one-pager with charts, not a formal PDF.”

---

### 25. **Add a Dose of Humor**

Even for serious topics:
“Explain GDPR using a Game of Thrones metaphor.”
The result? More memorable *and* understandable.

---

### 26. **Be a Director, Not a Dictator**

Think screenplay, not spreadsheet. You’re co-writing, not issuing orders. Add staging:
“Imagine you’re in a courtroom defending this idea…”

---

### 27. **Keep Your Prompts Modular**

Like LEGO. Use small, reusable pieces:

- Define the audience
- Set tone and format
- Add constraints
  Now you can mix and match across projects.

---

### 28. **Avoid Double Negatives**

Instead of “Don’t make it not too long,” just say “Keep it under 300 words.”
AI gets confused by unclear negation—just like humans do.

---

### 29. **Use Anchors and Labels**

Add clear labels like:

- **Audience**: Pediatricians
- **Tone**: Professional but warm
- **Output**: 500-word blog post
  This is like metadata for the prompt—AI reads and responds with precision.

---

### 30. **Stop Mid-Way for Feedback**

Say: “Pause halfway. Let me check the tone before you continue.”
You’ll avoid reading 4000 words that start great and end in a flaming blender of clichés.

---

### 31. **The Double Prompt Hack**

Send the *same* question twice—slightly reworded.
Compare responses. You’ll learn which phrasing the AI understands better. Use that structure moving forward.

---

### 32. **Debug Like a Developer**

Bad output? Don’t scream—iterate.
“What part of this prompt could be confusing?”
Try: “Let’s debug this together. What might improve the outcome?”

---

## Conclusion:

Asking an AI bot a question is like sending a carrier pigeon through a time vortex: you want it to return with gold, not gibberish. But the difference between genius and gibberish is usually in how you asked.

The future belongs to those who can prompt it.

So go forth, talk nerdy, and remember—if all else fails, just wrap it in `<tags>`. The bots will thank you.
